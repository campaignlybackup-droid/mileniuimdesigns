### Notes on the thin sections

Documents 04–08 add at least twelve more route files that appear nowhere in it: `/api/internal/market-snapshot` (04 §5.2), `/api/media/svg` (06 §7.1), `/api/admin/cms/pages/[id]/heartbeat` (06 §6.4), `/api/security/csp-report` (07 §5.8), `/api/catalog/products`, `/api/catalog/redirect-hit`, `/api/search`, `/api/search/click`, `/api/analytics/[market]/collect`, `/api/checkout/shipping-quote`, `/api/checkout/provider-authorization`, `/api/wishlist/summary`, `/api/account/summary`, `/api/feeds/[market]/*`, `/api/admin/*` (five routes), `/api/cron/pricing-rule-windows`. 08 §2.2 says `tests/unit/routes-authorized.test.ts` fails on "a route in the tree with no manifest row" — and 01 §3 is what an engineer scaffolds from. Also `src/lib/security/` (07's new folder) and `src/lib/giftcards/`, `src/lib/importexport/`, `src/lib/jobs/` are inconsistently present.

**E — Database schema** — THIN AS A DELIVERABLE. 02 declares itself "the canonical naming authority" and says "A name that appears here and differently elsewhere is a defect in the other document" — but 03–09 then add ~19 tables (`pricing_formulas`, `pricing_formula_versions`, `pricing_formula_market_terms`, `price_formula_bindings`, `variant_component_costs`, `tax_rules`, `checkout_sessions`, `checkout_gift_cards`, `order_events`, `payment_links`, `shipping_zones`, `shipping_zone_rules`, `shipping_methods`, `shipping_rates`, `curated_facet_markets`, `collection_rule_values`, `journal_categories`, `media_tags`, `content_preview_tokens`, `product_market_metrics`, `product_market_sort`, `back_in_stock_requests`, `redirect_hits`, `search_synonyms`, `search_promotions`, `search_redirects`) plus ~45 columns and ~25 indexes that never land back in 02. There is no consolidated DDL anywhere. An engineer writing the P03/P05/P10/P18 migrations from 02 alone ships a schema missing a third of the system; writing them by grepping six documents for `SCHEMA ADDITION` is the actual instruction, and nothing says so.

**F — Entity relationships** — THIN. The three ERDs cover only 02's own tables. None of the 26 tables added by 03–09 appears in any diagram — including `checkout_sessions` (the row every checkout request locates), `order_events` (the admin's primary screen), the four shipping tables, the four pricing-formula tables, and `product_market_sort`. §3.4's prose is excellent and is the part actually worth reading; the diagrams are already a partial map of a system that grew past them.

**R — Admin route map** — THIN where it matters most. Every route has a path and a permission, which is the security half. What is missing is the *screen* specification for the four admin features the brief calls out by name: bulk editing (no field set, no preview, no permission of its own), inline editing with save state (named only as a 10-design-system component), saved views (a table and a route, no filter/column/sharing UX), and global ⌘K search (a route with `dashboard.view`, no statement of what it searches, how it is indexed, or how results are permission-filtered). 06 spends 2,700 lines on the CMS admin; the catalogue/orders admin gets a route table.

## Cross-document contradictions

### C1 · CRITICAL · 03-catalog-architecture.md §2.4 vs 04-pricing-and-markets.md §1.3, §2.2–2.4

HYBRID AND METAL-LINKED PRICING ARE SPECIFIED TWICE, INCOMPATIBLY, AND ONE VERSION VIOLATES HARD RULE 6. 03 §2.4 puts formula evaluation ON THE READ PATH: "For a `metal_linked` or `hybrid` base row, step 4 is instead the §1.10 rule-2 expression, evaluated entirely in `bigint`, from `metal_rates` for that market's own currency" — inside the numbered `resolvePrice()` walk. 04 §1.3 states the opposite as a signature-level fact: "**`resolvePrice` never evaluates a formula.** This is the single most important consequence of hard rule 6 ... If the formula ran on read, every silver tick would move every live price and there would be nothing left for an admin to approve." 09 §1.2 P11 exit criterion (b) is literally "`resolvePrice` never calls `evaluateFormula`", and 09 §1.5 ranks this the 4th most expensive mistake in the project. Separately the two documents define `hybrid` differently — 03: "`hybrid` means **the metal-linked amount, floored at a manually set minimum**" with `prices.floor_minor` and `CHECK (chk_prices_hybrid_floor: price_source <> 'hybrid' OR floor_minor IS NOT NULL)`; 04 §2.4: "HYBRID is not a third algorithm. It is SILVER-LINKED plus one signed, stored, per-market number" with three `hybrid_adjustment_type` values and `CHECK chk_prices_hybrid_adjustment: (price_source = 'hybrid') = (hybrid_adjustment_delta_minor IS NOT NULL)`, putting the floor on `pricing_formula_market_terms.floor_minor` instead. 03's two CHECKs and 04's model cannot both be migrated: under 03's `chk_prices_floor_source: price_source = 'hybrid' OR floor_minor IS NULL`, 04's `metal_linked` rows with a market-terms floor are fine but every one of 04's hybrid rows is unwritable unless it also carries a `prices.floor_minor` that 04 never sets. The formulas also differ entirely (03 has no stone cost, no markup, no market adjustment, no component snapshot; 04 has six components plus `chk_prices_components_sum`). Discovering this at P11/P12 means rewriting the pricing service and re-migrating `prices` after price rows exist.

## Architectural disagreements

1. **The pricing formula system (04 §2.2) is over-built for a first release by a single developer, and it is the second-largest risk in the plan after checkout.** Four new tables (`pricing_formulas`, `pricing_formula_versions`, `pricing_formula_market_terms`, `price_formula_bindings`), thirteen snapshot columns on `prices`, two CHECK identities, a nano-minor arithmetic domain, `applyBpSigned`, three hybrid adjustment types with different recalculation semantics, and a seven-step approval workflow with two independent staleness windows — all to serve a NEEDS INPUT that 03 §10 admits may come back "silver only, manual entry": *"whether silver-linked, gold-linked or both, and the rate source (manual entry or a named vendor feed)"*, and 04 §9.2's own worked MANUAL example. The failing scenario is not that it is wrong — it is that P12 is on the critical path, M2 is "short, deep, dangerous" (09 §1.6), and the client may answer "manual only" after it is built. The alternative that keeps hard rule 6 intact at a fifth of the cost: ship `price_source ∈ {manual, metal_linked}`, one formula shape stored as columns on `price_formula_bindings` itself (material, weight source, purity source, making-charge mode + per-market amount, markup + per-market amount, rounding increment), the same `recalc_runs` approval machinery unchanged, and the six component snapshot columns unchanged. `hybrid` and the versioned-formula tables land when the client actually asks for a per-market override — which 04 §10's own NEEDS INPUT says may be never. The recalculation approval flow is the part that must ship on day one; the formula *authoring* system is the part that can be three columns.

2. **Two independent preview systems is one too many.** 04 §7.2 builds a signed-JWT middleware rewrite into an eight-file `(preview)` route group for market preview; 06 §4.4 builds a database-backed revocable token route for content preview, with its own table, passcode, view counter and scope rule. Both are well-argued in isolation and both solve "render unpublished/inactive content without poisoning an ISR object". The failing scenario is the intersection the client will hit in week one — previewing an unpublished CMS landing page in the not-yet-active India market — which neither system handles and which neither document mentions. One mechanism, `content_preview_tokens` with a nullable `market_code` (which 06 already has) plus `includeInactiveMarkets` in the grant, covers both and removes eight route files, one JWT audience, one middleware branch and one `robots.txt` prefix.

3. **The admin `(preview)` eight-file tree and the `/api/internal/market-snapshot` middleware fetch are both paying a real cost to avoid hardcoding two market codes for six weeks.** 04 §5.2's build-time snapshot + 60-second module cache + fail-open fetch + checksum test is a lot of machinery, and it is correct in principle — but 04 §5.2's own fallback is "the **build-time snapshot** written to `src/generated/market-snapshot.json` by the build step". If the build-time snapshot is acceptable as a cold-start answer, it is acceptable as the *only* answer until a third market exists: `generateStaticParams()` already queries `markets` at build time, adding a market already requires a `prices` row per variant (which is a deploy-adjacent event anyway), and `third-market.test.ts`'s "empty `git diff src/`" assertion still holds because `src/generated/` is gitignored (01 §5.9 step 5). This is one of the few places the set optimises for a requirement ("extend without a rebuild") past the point where the requirement is being tested.

4. **`product_market_sort` and `product_market_metrics` should be one table, and the argument in 08 §2.3 for why price sorting needs a derived table is the stronger one.** 08 §2.3 makes the correct and non-obvious case that `sort=price` has no stable single-column keyset value without a per-product rollup ("a product priced only at variant level has several, and the tuple `(price, id)` is then not unique per product"). 06 §2.1's `product_market_metrics` was written for a different reason (a CMS block sort) and duplicates `units_90d`. Merge them into `product_market_sort`, give it the transactional writer 08 specifies (`reindexProduct` inside the price-change transaction) and the nightly self-heal, and delete `product_metrics_refresh` from `job_kind`. Leaving both means two derived price/sales caches with different refresh guarantees, and 08 §3.2 already had to write a rule forbidding one of them from being read for a price — a rule that would not be needed if there were one table with one documented use.

5. **UNDER-BUILT: there is no admin reporting layer at all, and the brief's analytics requirements are a business deliverable, not a nice-to-have.** 08 §7.5 is eleven lines and specifies a source label. The gaps list covers the jewellery-specific reports; the architectural point is that no document names a reporting *module* — there is no `src/lib/reporting/`, no tier assignment, no query set, no per-market currency rule for aggregates, and no date-boundary rule against `markets.timezone`. Every other domain in this set gets a module, an owner, a signature list and a tier. Reporting gets a tile. For a client whose first question after launch will be "what sold", that is the largest asymmetry in the set.

6. **UNDER-BUILT: `cms_sections.background_token` and the surface vocabulary have no constraint, in a schema that CHECK-constrains eighteen other small TEXT value sets.** 02 §1.9 states the rule and applies it everywhere else ("Every one of them carries an explicit `CHECK (<col> IN (…))` listing its values ... Without it the set is documentation"). `background_token` is exactly that shape — a small, closed, merchant-selected value set local to one table — and it is the one place a designer-facing token can leak a hex value into the database, which 02 §2.8 explicitly forbids in prose. One CHECK, generated from the token list in 10 §2.1.

## Readiness verdict

An engineer can start P01–P02 tomorrow morning with no reservations, and P03 by the afternoon if they accept that the schema they are migrating lives in six files rather than one. The set is unusually strong where it matters most — the multi-market money model, the oversell guarantee, the webhook assertions, the SEO surface and the caching rules are each specified to the constraint name and the failing scenario, and 09's self-corrections show the documents have already been read against each other once. What is not ready is the *interface layer between documents*: roughly 26 tables, 20 permission keys, 15 error classes, 5 rate-limit keys, 4 job kinds and 5 route files exist in one document and not in the one that owns their vocabulary, and in several cases the owning document specifies a CI test that fails on exactly that divergence. The single most likely thing to go wrong first is not a bug at all — it is that P03A cannot pass its own exit criteria, because `tests/unit/rbac-catalogue.test.ts` and `tests/integration/rbac-matrix.test.ts` are specified to fail on any `PermissionKey` string literal in `src/` with no row in `catalogue.ts`, and 03, 04, 05 and 06 collectively require twenty such literals that 07's catalogue does not contain — including four (`cms_page.delete`, `cms_page.restore`, `media.hard_delete`, `media.update`) with no canonical equivalent at all, so the fix is a decision about the shape of the permission matrix rather than a rename. That will surface in M0, which is the cheapest possible place to find it. The two that will cost real money if they are *not* found in M0 are the pricing contradiction (03 §2.4 puts formula evaluation on the read path, which is hard rule 6 violated in the document an engineer builds the catalogue from, and it will not surface until someone enters a real silver rate) and the `send_email` job-kind closure in 07 §3.2, which silently fails every order-confirmation email from the first paid order. Before P03 starts, three hours spent reconciling the permission catalogue, the `job_kind` enum with its actor rule, the error taxonomy, and a single consolidated schema appendix would remove the majority of what is listed above; everything else on the contradiction list is discoverable by the tests these documents already commission.
